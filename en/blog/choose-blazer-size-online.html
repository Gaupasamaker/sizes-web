How to choose the right blazer size.

Check shoulders, chest, sleeves and length. Compare a garment that already fits and think about the layer you will wear underneath.

Updated 2026Adults · Blazers7 min read

A blazer can fasten comfortably and still pull when you reach forward. It can also look fine at the chest but sit too far beyond your shoulders. Before choosing a letter on the label, decide on the silhouette you want and compare the measurements that matter for that cut.

This guide focuses on lightweight transitional and office blazers, not structured winter coats. The brand's chart and the exact product page remain the references for your purchase.

Key takeaway

Check shoulders and chest first, then consider sleeve and body length. If you plan to wear a layer underneath, test the blazer with it on rather than sizing up by default.

Choose the fit you want before measuring

A tailored blazer, a straight cut and an oversized style are designed to create different silhouettes. Terms such as regular, relaxed and oversized are useful clues, but they are not a shared scale across retailers. Read the description for the exact style and look at its front and side views.

Think about how you will wear it, too. A blazer worn open over a T-shirt can feel different from one you plan to fasten over a shirt. Set that goal before comparing sizes.

Keep body measurements separate from garment measurements

If the size guide asks for body measurements, take yours as instructed and compare them with its chart. To compare a garment, find a blazer in your wardrobe with a similar cut and fabric that fits the way you like. Lay it flat without stretching and measure from consistent points:

  • Shoulders: from one shoulder seam to the other.
  • Chest: flat, from pit to pit, with the blazer fastened.
  • Sleeve: from the shoulder seam to the cuff.
  • Length: from the base of the collar to the hem.

A garment's flat width is not your body circumference. Do not double a measurement unless the guide explicitly tells you to convert it.

Start with shoulders and chest

On a classic cut, the shoulder seam often sits near the edge of the shoulder; on a dropped or oversized pattern, it may intentionally sit lower. Compare like with like rather than applying one universal rule. If the seam pulls, forms strong creases or limits your arms, review the chart and cut before deciding.

Fasten the blazer, reach forward and give yourself a gentle hug. It should have the ease intended by its design, without uncomfortable tension or more fabric than you wanted. Product photos can suggest a silhouette, but they cannot replace measurements.

Test the room with the layer you plan to wear

If you will wear a shirt, use that shirt for the fit check. If you plan to add a lightweight jumper, put it on as well: extra volume changes the feel around the chest, armhole and sleeves. Check that you can move comfortably and that the blazer keeps the shape you want.

Sizing up may create more room, but it can also lengthen the sleeves or shift the shoulders. If one area feels tight, check the pattern description and garment measurements instead of assuming the whole size needs to change.

Set sleeve and body length to your preference

A sleeve may cover the wrist or leave more of a shirt cuff visible, depending on the styling and design. Body length also varies between cropped, straight and longer blazers. Compare it with a garment you like and check the product photos from different angles.

If you are between options, identify where the difference matters—shoulders, chest, sleeve or length. One size up will not correct every point at once.

If you are between sizes

Start with the brand's chart. If your measurements point to different sizes, look for the brand's advice on which area to prioritise for that garment and consider how much ease the cut is meant to have. If there is no clear instruction, decide which area needs the most comfort and compare the measurements for both options.

Before buying, check the fabric composition, lining and exchange or return terms. For more context, read why sizes vary between brands and how to choose a knitwear size.

Before you add it to your cart

  • Decide whether you will wear it open or fastened.
  • Make sure you have the guide for the right category and style.
  • Compare body measurements with the chart and garment measurements with a reference blazer.
  • Check shoulders, chest, sleeves and length.
  • Test it with your planned layer and move your arms.
  • Do not size up automatically because a style is labelled oversized.

Your most useful reference is not the letter you usually buy, but the combination of measurements, pattern and use that feels right. Save those details so you can use them when comparing another brand.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose a blazer size online?

Compare your body measurements with the brand's chart and check them against a blazer that fits the way you want. Review shoulders, chest, sleeves and length with your planned layer.

Should I size up to wear a jumper underneath?

Not automatically. Test whether the cut leaves enough room with the jumper on; sizing up can also change the shoulders and sleeves.

Does oversized mean I should buy a larger size?

No. Oversized describes the intended pattern, not an instruction to size up. Check the product page and measurements for that style.

Which measurement should I prioritise between two sizes?

First follow the brand's advice for that garment. If it does not settle the question, identify where you need more comfort and compare the actual measurements of both sizes.
