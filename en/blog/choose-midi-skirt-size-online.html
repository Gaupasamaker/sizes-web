How to choose a midi skirt size online.

Waist and hip measurements are a starting point; the cut, fabric and length help explain how each style may fit.

Updated 26 Oct 2026Women · Skirts7 min read

The same size can produce very different results in a straight, A-line or pleated midi skirt. The label is only a guide: fit depends on where the waistband sits, how much room the hips need and how the fabric moves.

Before ordering, use your body measurements with the brand's chart, then read the details for the exact style. If you already own a skirt that fits the way you like, use it as an extra reference for silhouette and length.

Key point

Start with waist and hips, then decide with the pattern and length in mind. Do not compare a body measurement with a skirt laid flat until you know what each figure represents.

Measure your waist and hips consistently

Use a flexible tape over underwear or a thin layer, standing naturally without pulling it tight. For your waist, follow the brand's diagram: it may indicate the narrowest part of your torso or a specific line. For your hips, wrap the tape around the fullest area and keep it level. Record both measurements in centimetres.

Brands do not always place a skirt's waist at the same height. A high-rise style can sit at a narrower point than a low-rise one; a firm waistband also allows less variation than an elastic one. Check the product description and look for darts, gathers or a side fastening.

A body size chart and a garment measurement table are different things. The first maps body measurements to a size; the second describes the finished skirt. If both are available, identify which table you are reading. Size advice can vary with a style's cut and fabric, so follow the guidance shown on the exact product page.

The cut changes how much room you need

A straight skirt often depends more on the fit through the hips and lower part. An A-line style can sit closer at the top and flare below. Pleats add movement, but they do not automatically mean the waistband or hips have extra room.

Read terms such as straight, pencil, A-line, pleated and fluid as descriptions of shape, not standardised size labels. Front and side photos help you identify the pattern. If the page lists the garment's waist or hip measurements, compare them with a similar skirt you own and like.

Construction matters too: an elasticated waistband may adjust more than a fixed one; a slit can make a narrow cut easier to walk in, but it does not change the hip measurement. Fastenings, lining and fibre content are useful clues, although they cannot tell you exactly how much a particular garment will stretch.

Check the length and where the hem will fall

“Midi” describes an in-between length, not one precise point shared by every brand. If the product page gives the skirt length, check where the measurement starts: the waist, the top of the waistband or the side seam. Then compare it with a skirt you own, laid flat with the waistband in a similar position.

The model's height and the size she wears can add context when the brand provides them. They cannot predict the result on another body: torso and leg proportions, waist placement and footwear all differ. Think about where you want the hem to sit, then use the product measurements to get closer.

If length is your main concern, prioritise a listing with measurements in centimetres or compare with a skirt of similar length. Sizing up is not a precise way to add length: it may add room at the waist or hips without solving the hem position.

When your waist and hips suggest different sizes

There is no single rule for every skirt. First find out where the style is meant to fit and whether its fabric has stretch. With a fixed waistband and straight cut, the hips may be the limiting area; with an elastic waist, the waist may allow more variation. Follow the specific product recommendation if one is given.

If possible, compare a similar skirt you own. Measure the waistband without stretching it and the widest part of the hips. Do not pull an elastic garment to its maximum to get a number: that will not tell you how it feels throughout the day. If you are between sizes, consider which area needs room and what changes in length or volume the next size brings.

A quick pre-purchase checklist

  • Measure your waist and hips using the brand's diagram.
  • Check whether the chart describes your body or the finished garment.
  • Identify the rise, waistband construction and skirt cut.
  • Compare the listed length with a skirt you already own.
  • Use model height and size as context, not a guarantee.
  • If you are between sizes, first work out which area needs room.

Choosing a midi skirt online is a comparison of measurements and preferences, not a search for one perfect letter. Keep a note of the size you tried, how the waist felt and where the hem landed; that will be more useful than the label alone next time.

Frequently asked questions

What measurements do I need to choose a midi skirt?

Start with your waist and hips using the brand's instructions. Also check the garment length, cut and waistband construction.

What size should I choose if my waist and hips point to different sizes?

It depends on the pattern, fabric and where the skirt fits closely. Check that product's recommendation and compare a similar garment; do not automatically size up or down.

Will a midi skirt fall at the same point on everyone?

No. Garment length and proportions affect where the hem falls. Model height is context; compare the listed centimetres when available.

Will sizing up make the skirt longer?

Not necessarily. It may add room at the waist or hips without changing the length much. Check the measurements for each size and the product details.
