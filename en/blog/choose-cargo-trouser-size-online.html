How to choose your cargo trouser size online.

Waist, hip, rise and length: compare measurements and cut before deciding whether you want a straight, relaxed or closer fit.

Updated 2026Adults · Trousers7 min read

Side pockets give cargo trousers their character, but they do not answer the important question: how do they fit at the waist, hip and leg? Before choosing a size, decide on the silhouette you want and compare the cut of the specific style, not just the letter on the label.

This method works for everyday and transitional-season cargos. The brand's size guide and the measurements on the product page come first, because cut, fabric and available sizing vary from one item to another.

Key idea

Use the brand's chart for your body measurements and a reference garment for comparing garment measurements. Do not treat flat width as a body circumference or size up just because of the pockets.

Choose the silhouette first

Cargo trousers may be straight, tapered, relaxed, wide-leg or cuffed. These labels are useful clues, but they do not describe identical patterns across brands. Read the product details and check the front and side views; notice where the hem falls and how much volume the leg has.

Think about how you will wear them. For walking or sitting for long periods, you may prefer more room through the thigh and knee; for a cleaner line, you may prefer a straight or tapered leg. The goal is not simply to buy “larger”, but to get the movement you need and the shape you like.

Keep body measurements and garment measurements separate

If the brand provides a body chart, measure your waist and hips according to its instructions and compare those figures with the stated range. If the product page gives garment measurements, compare them with a pair you own that fits well and has a similar cut. Do not compare your body's circumference directly with a garment laid flat.

To measure a reference pair, fasten it, lay it flat without stretching it and use the same points each time:

  • Waist: measure straight across the top of the waistband.
  • Hip: measure across the widest area, usually below the fly.
  • Rise: measure from the crotch seam to the top of the waistband.
  • Thigh: measure across the leg close to the crotch, using the same point on both pairs.
  • Inside leg: follow the inside seam from the crotch to the hem.

Construction can make two pairs in the same labelled size feel different. Note whether your reference has stretch, an elastic waist, pleats or a different rise.

Start with waist and hip

The waistband should sit where the design intends without digging in when you sit or slipping as you walk. Check the hip with the trousers fastened and look for strain lines, pockets that pull open or folds that do not belong to the intended shape.

If your measurements fall between two ranges, return to the brand's guide and see which measurement it advises you to prioritise. Do not automatically reuse a size from another category: trousers, jeans and unisex styles may use different references.

Check rise and thigh while moving

Rise affects where the waistband sits and how much room you feel through the crotch. A lower rise may pull when you sit; a higher one may feel different even if waist and hip measurements match. Read the product description and compare it with trousers that have a similar rise.

Try a simple movement test: sit down, take a few steps and bend your knees. Check that the fabric does not restrict you and that the waistband does not shift uncomfortably. If the thigh feels tight while the waist already fits, sizing up may solve one area and leave another too loose.

Match the length to the hem

The right length depends on your footwear and the finish you want. A straight hem may rest on your trainers; a cuffed or drawcord cargo may be adjusted differently. Compare the inside leg with a reference pair and treat the model's height as visual context, not a size recommendation.

Pockets are not a size recommendation

Cargo pockets add fabric at the sides and can create more visual volume, especially when filled. That does not mean you need a larger size. Check the fit through the hip and thigh with the pockets empty, then consider how you plan to use them.

Fabric matters too: a rigid cloth holds its shape, while stretch may move more easily with you. Check the composition and care details, but do not assume every cargo style from one brand fits the same way.

If you are between sizes

First identify the area causing doubt: waist, hip, rise, thigh or length. Use the body chart to choose a size and garment measurements to understand the ease. If the brand gives a recommendation for that exact item, read it alongside the cut and fabric; do not apply it to another style.

For more context, see how to choose your jeans size online, what to do when you are between sizes and why sizes change between brands.

Before you buy

  • Decide on the silhouette and how you will wear the trousers.
  • Check the guide for the exact category and product.
  • Compare body measurements with the chart and one garment with another.
  • Review waist, hip, rise, thigh and length.
  • Test your range of movement before choosing more room.
  • Do not size up automatically because of cargo pockets or a “relaxed” label.

A good choice combines measurements with real use. Keep a reference pair that fits the way you like, and you will have something more useful than a letter when comparing your next pair of cargos.

Frequently asked questions

Which measurements matter for cargo trousers?

Start with waist and hip, then check rise, thigh and length for the cut. Use the brand's body chart and compare garment measurements with another garment measured in the same way.

Do cargo pockets mean I should size up?

No. They add volume to the design but do not determine the size. Check waist, hip and thigh, then test your movement.

How do I compare a trouser's flat width with my measurements?

Do not compare flat width with your body circumference. Compare garment with garment at equivalent points, and body with the brand's chart.

What if the waist fits but the thigh feels tight?

Check the cut and garment measurements first. Sizing up may leave the waist too loose, so look for a style with more room through the thigh if available.
