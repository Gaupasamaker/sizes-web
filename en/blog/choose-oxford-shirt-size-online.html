How to choose the right Oxford shirt size online.

Shoulders, chest, collar and sleeves: compare the shirt measurements with the cut and layers you plan to wear.

Updated 9 Nov 2026Adults · Shirts8 min read

An Oxford shirt can look classic in a product photo and still fit in very different ways: straighter or closer through the body, with a defined or dropped shoulder, and shorter for wearing untucked or cut to tuck in. The size label alone will not tell you how it will sit on you.

Before choosing a size, decide how you plan to wear it. A buttoned shirt under a blazer needs a different feel from an open Oxford over a T-shirt on a mild autumn day. Then compare your measurements with the chart for the exact garment.

Key idea

Check what each chart measures and compare the shirt with a garment that already fits the way you like. Do not carry a size across brands without checking the cut and measurements.

Start with the fit you want

“Oxford” describes a fabric and a recognisable shirt construction; it does not define one universal silhouette. Product pages may offer slim, regular, relaxed or oversized fits. Two shirts with the same label can have different widths and lengths.

Think about how you will wear it before measuring: buttoned up, open, tucked in or under a light jumper? If it is a layer, leave room for the T-shirt you will actually wear. For a shirt worn on its own or beneath a blazer, you may prefer the shoulder seam closer to its natural position.

Read the product chart, not just the label

A chart may show body measurements, finished garment measurements, or both. Do not compare a body measurement with a shirt laid flat: they describe different things. Open the exact colour and style you plan to buy, then look for notes on fit, length, fabric and measurements by size.

Official product pages show why the detail matters. A UNIQLO Oxford shirt lists garment measurements, and UNIQLO’s size help explains how to check each item’s chart. An H&M Regular Fit Oxford shirt describes its fit and lists measurements for the selected size. These are examples of specific product pages, not every item from either brand.

Check shoulders and chest

To measure your body, wrap the tape around the fullest part of your chest, keep it level and do not pull it tight. Follow the brand’s guide: some charts also ask for neck or waist measurements. Record centimetres and repeat if the tape slips or tilts.

To compare a shirt you own, button it and lay it flat without stretching the fabric. Measure shoulder seam to shoulder seam and across the chest at the point shown on the product chart. If the number is a flat width, do not compare it directly with a body circumference.

When trying it on, check whether the shoulder seam sits where you want it and whether the buttons lie flat when you breathe and sit down. If they pull or gape, review the chest and cut before assuming you need a larger size everywhere.

Account for the collar and fastening

Some shirts use letter sizes; others include a neck measurement. Check which system the product uses: a neck number does not automatically translate to an S, M or L. If the page lists neck circumference, measure it as instructed.

With the collar buttoned, it should feel comfortable when you swallow and turn your head. If you usually wear the shirt open, shoulders, chest and sleeves may matter more for your intended use.

Check sleeve and body length in your usual posture

Product pages may measure a sleeve from the shoulder seam or from the centre back. Check the starting point before comparing two garments. Measure a shirt you own using the same method and without pulling the sleeve.

A shirt you plan to tuck in needs enough length to stay in place when you sit; if you plan to wear it untucked, check where the hem falls. Raise and cross your arms to see whether it allows the movement you need.

Set the ease for autumn layering

An Oxford shirt can work with a T-shirt, a fine jumper or a light jacket. Use the combination you will actually wear and test the movement: button the shirt, sit down, reach forward and add the outer layer if relevant. You will see whether tension appears at the shoulders, back, chest or sleeves.

Do not size up just to gain room in one area. A larger size can also lengthen the sleeves and widen the body. If the style comes in another cut, compare its measurements before changing size.

If you are between sizes

Confirm that you opened the chart for the right category and that the measurements refer to the body or garment. Then decide which area matters most for your intended use. A shirt in your wardrobe can be a useful reference if its fabric and cut are similar.

Save the brand, style, size and the area that felt comfortable or tight. That specific note is more useful than remembering a letter on its own.

Before you add it to your cart

  • Decide whether you will wear it alone, open or as a layer.
  • Check whether the chart shows body, garment or both.
  • Compare shoulders, chest, sleeves and length using the same method.
  • Check whether collar size is listed separately.
  • Read the exact fit and product notes.
  • If possible, move around in the layers you plan to wear.

Choosing an Oxford shirt online means bringing measurements, pattern and use together. Save the exact product page and how it fitted so your next purchase starts from a real reference, not a label alone.

Frequently asked questions

How should an Oxford shirt fit?

It depends on whether you want a close, regular or relaxed cut and how you will wear it. Check the shoulders, chest and sleeves, then move around in the buttoned shirt to judge the room.

How can I tell whether a chart measures the body or the shirt?

Read the headings and the retailer’s explanation. Measurements for a garment laid flat cannot be compared directly with your body circumference.

Can I use my Oxford shirt size for another brand?

Use it as a starting point, then check the chart and cut of the exact garment. The same letter does not guarantee the same dimensions.

What if the chest fits but the sleeves are too long?

Check whether that style offers a sleeve-length option or another cut. Changing size may also affect the chest and shoulders.
