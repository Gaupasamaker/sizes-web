How to choose the right size in corduroy trousers online.

Compare your waist, hips and leg length with the cut and composition of the exact style; your usual label size is only part of the picture.

Updated 2 Nov 2026Women · Trousers8 min read

Two pairs of corduroy trousers can use the same fabric and still fit differently. One may sit high on the waist and fit closely through the hips; another may have a straight or wide leg. To shop with fewer doubts, combine your measurements with the cut and the exact product details.

Corduroy describes a ribbed fabric, not one standard shape. Check the composition, waistband and measurements published for each style. If you already own trousers you like, use them as an extra reference and compare the same points.

Key point

Start with your body measurements and confirm the fit against the product page. Do not automatically convert one brand’s numbered size into another brand’s size.

Measure where the waistband will sit

Measure your waist at the height where you plan to wear the trousers; that may not be the narrowest part of your torso. Measure your hips as the brand instructs, keeping the tape level around the fullest area. Note the measurements in centimetres and check whether the chart refers to your body or to the finished garment.

If you compare them with trousers you already own, lay the garment flat without stretching it. Measure the waistband from side to side, the front rise from the crotch seam to the top of the waistband, and the inside leg from that seam to the hem. Check how the retailer presents each figure: a flat measurement and a circumference are different things.

A model’s height and the size they wear can add context when the product page provides them. They cannot predict the result on another body; proportions, waistband position and cut all matter too.

Read the composition together with the cut

Composition helps explain how a garment is built, but it is not enough to choose a size. As current examples, Levi’s describes its 728 High-Rise Wide-Leg corduroy trousers as high-rise, close through the hip and relaxed through the thigh; its product page lists 98% cotton and 2% elastane. H&M describes its cotton corduroy trousers as high-waisted with a straight leg, also with 98% cotton and 2% elastane.

The two pages show why similar composition does not make two patterns equivalent. Levi’s lists a model who is 170 cm tall with a 69 cm waist wearing size 27 x 32. For its size 38, H&M lists a garment waist circumference of 76.3 cm and a 78.5 cm inside leg, as well as the model’s height. These figures belong to those product pages; they do not create a size conversion between brands.

Look for terms such as “straight”, “wide leg”, “relaxed”, “fitted” or “high rise” and check which area they describe. If a product page mentions stretch, treat that as a fabric detail, not a promise that any size will work.

Choose the silhouette you want first

With a cut that fits closely through the hip, that area may determine how comfortable the trousers feel when you sit. A wide leg gives the thigh more room, but the waistband can still be close. “Straight leg” does not mean the same amount of ease in every brand.

Think about how you will wear them: at your natural waist or lower, with a top tucked in or out, and with which shoes. Then review the front and side photos and look for waistband, hip and length measurements. If the page does not provide a figure, do not infer it from the photo.

The size label is not a universal measure of leg length either. Some pages let you choose waist and length separately; others publish the length for one size. Going up a size is not a precise way to lengthen the leg: it can add volume elsewhere.

Check the fit in motion

If you have the trousers at home, try them with the underwear you would normally wear. Sit down, take a few steps and climb a stair. Notice whether the waistband digs in, seams pull across the hip or thigh, or the fabric restricts movement. Check the length with the shoes you plan to wear.

If you compare with another garment, choose one with a similar cut. Cargo trousers with roomy pockets or stretchy jeans can help you compare some measurements, but they are not an exact template for a different pair of corduroy trousers.

If you are between sizes

First, measure again and make sure you are reading the right chart. Then identify the area that concerns you: waist, hip, thigh or length. Check whether the exact product page offers a fit recommendation and whether the waistband or closure allows any room.

Do not automatically size up. The next size may add room where you do not need it and change the drape. If you are still unsure, compare a similar garment you own and note which size you tried and how it moved with you. Our guide to choosing between two sizes can help you think it through.

Before you add them to your cart

  • Measure your waist and hips using the retailer’s guide.
  • Check whether each figure describes your body or the garment.
  • Identify the rise, cut and composition of the exact style.
  • Compare the rise and length with similar trousers that fit well.
  • Try sitting and walking if you can test them at home.
  • Save the brand, style, size and the areas that felt comfortable or tight.

Choosing corduroy trousers online means combining measurements, cut and use. Save the style and what you learned from trying it on: that specific reference will help more next time than a size label on its own.

Frequently asked questions

What measurements do I need for corduroy trousers?

Start with waist and hips using the brand’s guide. If you compare with a pair you own, also measure the front rise and inside leg at the same points described on the new product page.

Will corduroy stretch as I wear it?

That depends on the composition and construction of each pair. Check whether the page lists elastane or stretch, and do not assume that every corduroy garment will behave in the same way.

Can I use my jeans size as a reference?

Use it as a starting point, not a conversion. Compare waist, hip, rise, length and cut with the specific corduroy trousers you want to buy.

What should I do if I am between sizes?

Check your measurements and decide which area needs comfort. Review the exact product recommendation and compare a similar cut before sizing up or down.
