How to choose a rain jacket size for layering.

A mid-layer changes the room you need, but it does not make “size up” a rule. Measure, check the cut and try the combination you will wear.

Updated 2026Adults · Layers8 min read

A rain jacket can zip comfortably over a T-shirt and pull across your back once you add a fleece. The opposite happens too: you buy a larger size “just in case” and end up with long sleeves, dropped shoulders and a hem that shifts whenever you raise your arms.

Start by separating three things: your measurements, the model's cut and the layers you will really wear underneath. The label helps you find a size range; by itself, it does not tell you how much room the jacket has or how it will move with you.

Key takeaway

Choose from the chart and fit notes for the specific model. Try it with your planned layer and check the shoulders, sleeves and movement before removing the tags. Do not size up without knowing which area needs more room.

First, decide what kind of jacket you need

An uninsulated waterproof shell is designed to sit over the clothes you are already wearing; it does not have the built-in bulk of a padded coat. A lined or insulated jacket, or a 3-in-1 system, works differently. Before measuring, check whether the product is a lightweight outer layer, an insulated garment or a set with an inner piece.

Think about the activity too. For a brisk walk or cycling, you may prefer a closer shape that does not flap. For a hike with a thick fleece, check the chest, back and arms with that layer on. There is no single amount of ease that works for every model and activity.

Take the measurements the brand asks for

Use a flexible tape over a thin shirt. Keep it level and snug without pulling it tight. The chart may ask for chest, hip and arm length. Follow the brand's own measuring instructions: a measurement taken differently can put you in the wrong row.

Write down the centimetres as well as the letter size. If the chart gives a range, note where each measurement falls. If one sits right on a boundary, measure again. Some brands explain which measurement to prioritise if chest and hip point to different sizes. Follow that advice and read the model's fit notes before choosing.

A body chart is not a chart of the finished jacket's dimensions. It also cannot tell you sleeve length or hem width if those figures are not listed. For those details, check the product page or ask the brand.

Try the layer you plan to wear

Choose a realistic combination: the T-shirt and fleece you expect to wear most often. Zip the jacket up and keep the tags on. If you only need a thin base layer day to day, do not test it over a bulky jumper you will rarely wear. If the jacket is meant for winter, do not judge it over a T-shirt alone.

Raise your arms, reach forward, cross them and turn your torso. The back should not pull so much that the hem rides up, and the shoulder seam should not shift uncomfortably. You should be able to close the zip without compressing the inner layer or stretching the pockets open.

Then relax your arms. The jacket should not stay bunched up or create so much excess fabric under the arms that it catches. Useful ease lets you move as planned while keeping the cuffs, sleeves and hem where they belong.

Shoulders, sleeves and hem reveal an oversized fit

When you go up a size, the chest is not the only thing that changes. Sleeves may cover part of your hand, the shoulders may drop and the hem may sit lower. Adjustable cuffs can close around a sleeve, but they cannot fix proportions that make it hard to use your hands or carry a pack.

Check the length with your arms down and then extended. The cuff should still cover your wrist without riding too far up when you reach. Adjust it as you would outdoors, then turn your head: the hood should move with you without pulling the collar backwards.

Read the model's fit notes, not just the brand name

Words such as slim, regular and relaxed describe design intent, not universal measurements. A technical shell with a closer cut and an urban raincoat from the same brand may leave very different room over the chest in the same labelled size.

On the product page, look for front and side photos, the model's height and worn size, and any note about layering or fit. Treat those details as context, not a conversion: two people of the same height can have different proportions.

If you are between sizes, find out why

Does the chest feel close while the sleeves are already long? Does the fleece pull at your shoulders although the body has room? Does the jacket only feel tight when you reach forward? Each case points to a different area. Sizing up might solve the chest and make the sleeves and length worse.

Compare both options with the same layer. If the smaller size allows free movement and the larger one only adds volume, you may not need to change. If the smaller size clearly restricts a measurement the brand says to prioritise, try the next size and check all the proportions again. If both sizes fail in different places, that cut may not be right for you.

A quick fitting check before you buy

  • Confirm whether you are buying a shell, an insulated jacket or a 3-in-1 system.
  • Measure the areas listed in the official chart and note the centimetres.
  • Read the fit of that model; do not treat it as the fit of the whole brand.
  • Try the jacket with the layer you will actually wear.
  • Zip it up and raise, cross and extend your arms.
  • Check the shoulder, cuff, sleeve length, hem and hood.
  • Review the return policy before wearing it outside.

Save the model and layer with your size

“Size M” does not explain why a jacket worked. “Size M, regular fit, comfortable over a T-shirt and light fleece, sleeves slightly long” gives you a useful starting point for next time. Save the model and the measurements you used as well.

Frequently asked questions

Should I buy a size up to wear a fleece underneath?

Not automatically. Check the specific model's fit and try it with the fleece you plan to wear. Make sure you can move without the shoulders or sleeves shifting out of place.

Which measurements should I check for a rain jacket?

Start with the body measurements requested by the official chart, especially chest, hip and arm if listed. Then check the fit described for the specific model.

How should a rain jacket fit?

It should allow your planned layer and movement without pulling at the shoulders or back, while keeping the sleeves and hem in position.

What should I do if I am between sizes?

Identify which measurement is at the limit and check the cut. Decide after trying your planned layer; sizing up can also add sleeve length and overall volume.
