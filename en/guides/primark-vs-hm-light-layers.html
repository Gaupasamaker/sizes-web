Primark vs H&M: which size to choose in basics and light layers.

A T-shirt, shirt and overshirt can share a label and still feel very different. Compare the actual garment, its composition and the room you need before transferring a size between brands.

Updated 20269 min readAdult · Transitional
Our advice

Do not convert a Primark size directly into an H&M size. Start with the garment type, check the layer underneath and use shoulders, bust, waist and length as references. Official charts help, but the current product chart matters most.

What the official size guides can tell you

Primark's size guide includes body measurements, international conversions and category advice. It also notes that sizing and cut can vary as the brand improves the fit of individual garments.

H&M explains in its size guide that sizing information is accessed from the product page. That matters: the reference can change with the item, collection and cut.

These sources help with the purchase in front of you, not with a permanent conversion. Keep an older size as a clue, then confirm the current chart, composition and fit description.

T-shirts: start with the first layer

A jersey T-shirt can tolerate a closer fit than a woven shirt. If it will sit underneath another garment, check that the shoulder is not already at its limit and that the hem does not create difficult bulk. The length should work both loose and under the outer layer.

Primark and H&M both offer fitted, regular and roomy cuts. Do not compare the label alone. Compare bust, shoulders and length with a T-shirt that fits and note whether you will wear it alone or with another layer.

Shirts: the shoulder separates size from proportion

Fasten the shirt and cross your arms. Buttons should stay flat, the back should follow movement and the armhole should not cut in. If the smaller size fits the shoulders but pulls across the bust, sizing up may make sense; if the larger one solves the bust but drops the shoulder, look for another cut.

A relaxed shirt already includes ease. A larger size may add sleeves and length without improving the fall. Read the description and check for a dropped shoulder, darts, a wide back or a raglan sleeve.

Overshirts: layer without losing the shape

An overshirt should allow the layer beneath and keep a reasonable shoulder line. Try it over the real T-shirt, raise your arms and sit down. If the body rides up or the back pulls, you need more range; if only the hem feels long, the next size is not automatically the answer.

SIZES tip: save a note such as “M, twill overshirt, good shoulders, long sleeves, comfortable over a T-shirt”. That context is more useful than “M in the brand”.

Fabric changes the feeling

Jersey cotton usually follows movement more than poplin. Light denim keeps its body, and a fine sweatshirt can have volume even when it feels soft. Composition gives you a direction, but it does not predict size by itself: also check construction and layer thickness.

Do not expect a garment to stretch into a good fit if it pulls on the first try. Do not deliberately buy large for a supposed shrinkage either. Follow the care instructions and choose a fit that works today.

Trousers: waist, hips and rise

A waistband closing does not prove the trousers have enough room. If pockets open, lines appear under the zip or the rise limits you when sitting, check hips, thigh and rise. With wide styles, separate leg volume from the upper fit.

Elastic waists allow more variation, but they should support without digging in and recover their shape. Sizing up may make the hem drag without solving a short rise. Compare with trousers of similar structure.

The same label does not distribute space in the same way

Two M garments can target similar body ranges and place their volume in different areas. A T-shirt may add room through the torso; a shirt may keep a precise armhole. Trousers may have a wide leg and a short rise.

Look at vertical measurements too. Torso length changes where a waist sits, rise changes how the hips are distributed and a long sleeve may show that sizing up will move the shoulder. If the circumference works but the seams do not, proportion may be the problem.

Reviews and fit descriptions

A review helps when it says “short sleeve”, “wide shoulder”, “firm waist” or “non-stretch fabric”. Do not copy another person's size without their measurements, but use details to decide what to inspect.

Model height and displayed size help interpret length and volume. They do not replace the current chart. Combine the model, composition, measurements and a garment of your own with comparable structure.

Common mistakes when comparing Primark and H&M

The first is converting one brand's letter into the other's. The second is comparing a fitted T-shirt with a relaxed overshirt. The third is sizing up to solve one tight spot and accepting sleeves or lengths that are out of proportion.

It is also common to treat “oversize” as if it meant the item simply runs large. Volume can be designed and balanced by the shoulder, cuff and length. Sizing up changes the whole pattern.

If you are between sizes

Write down what fails in each option. In a shirt, the smaller size may pull at the bust and the larger one may drop the shoulder. In trousers, the smaller may restrict the hips and the larger may open at the waist. Choose the option that keeps the seams balanced and allows your real use.

If neither size places the main areas correctly, change the cut. An adjustable waist, a different rise or more bust room can solve what a larger size cannot.

The movement test

With the tags still on, use the garment as you would during the day. Raise and cross your arms in tops; sit and step up in trousers; turn your torso in an overshirt. Check the front, sides and back.

Buttons should stay flat, seams should return to place and pockets should not open under tension. Check return conditions before washing, altering or removing tags.

How to use SIZES

Save body measurements and add a garment note: “Primark M, regular shirt, good bust fit” or “H&M M, relaxed overshirt, comfortable over a T-shirt”. The size keeps its context and does not become a rule for the whole brand.

Final recommendation

There is no single way for a brand to fit every garment. Specific items work better with specific measurements, ease preferences and intended uses. Check the current information, compare it with your own reference and test movement before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Do Primark and H&M basics fit the same?

Do not assume it. Fit depends on the garment, cut, fabric and specific collection. Check the current product guide.

Should I size up to wear layers?

Not always. Check the layer underneath, shoulders, bust and movement before choosing a larger size.

What if a size fits the bust but the sleeves are too long?

Prioritise the proportion you cannot correct and look for another cut if sizing up adds too much volume.
