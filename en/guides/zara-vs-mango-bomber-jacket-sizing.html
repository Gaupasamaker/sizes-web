Zara vs Mango: bomber jacket sizing.

We compare each brand's official guidance and two specific bomber styles with different cuts. The header image is an editorial illustration, not a product-catalog photo.

Our advice

Do not convert a Zara size directly into a Mango size. Start with your body measurements, use each brand's guide, then check the cut and measurements of the specific bomber jacket.

What this guide does and does not compare

The scope is women's outerwear and bomber-style jackets. We are not claiming that one brand always fits larger or smaller, or that all products from either brand share a fit. The useful comparison is how to use the right guide and read each product page.

A cropped bomber, a straight wool style and a raised-collar suede-effect jacket can feel different even within one shop. The specific examples show what information to check; they do not establish a general Zara or Mango sizing rule.

Zara: check the cut and the tool for each item

Zara's official size guidance says fit can vary by style, cut and fabric. Each product page can include a measurement guide and, where available, Find your size, which uses the information you provide about your measurements and how you like to wear clothes.

As one example, the suede-effect bomber jacket listed on Zara Spain has a raised collar, flap-front pockets, ribbed trims and a zip fastening. The checked page shows a 176 cm model but does not state her size. The image helps show the silhouette; it cannot tell you which size will fit your body.

Open the guide for the exact item and check its measurements by size. If the cut is roomier or shorter than you want, compare the chart and product photos before choosing.

Mango: compare the Measurements section with your own

Mango's official size help directs shoppers to the Measurements section, where the measurements for each size can be compared with their own. Check that the information belongs to the product and category you want.

The wool bomber jacket in our example has a straight cut, stand-up collar, elastic cuffs and lining. Its product page says the model is wearing size S and is 5 ft 10 in tall. Treat that as context for the styling on that product page, not as a universal recommendation.

If the bomber you want is oversized, cropped or made from another fabric, check its own description. Do not assume the wool jacket's model information applies to every Mango bomber.

Compare your body with the chart and one garment with another

Body charts and finished-garment measurements answer different questions. Measure yourself as the brand instructs; then, for a second reference, compare a jacket you own that fits well with the product dimensions, using the same points.

For a bomber, check shoulders, chest, sleeve and body length. Measure with the jacket fastened and without stretching it. The intended ease is part of the pattern: a larger chest measurement does not necessarily mean the brand expects you to size up.

An elastic waistband, ribbed trims, lining and fabric thickness can affect how the jacket feels over another layer. Check those details in the product composition and photos.

Test movement and the layer you plan to wear

With the jacket on, zip it up, cross your arms, reach forward and sit down. Check whether the shoulders pull, the chest feels comfortable and the sleeves stay where you want them. If you will wear a light jumper underneath, repeat the test with that layer.

Do not automatically size up for extra room: a larger size may also lengthen the sleeves and body or shift the shoulders. First identify which area needs more ease, then compare the specific style's measurements.

If you are between sizes

Check the recommendation on the exact product page and see where each measurement sits against the chart. Decide what you want to prioritise — shoulders, chest, sleeves or length — and compare the garment's cut. If the information is not enough, review the exchange and return terms before buying.

Before you add it to your basket

  • Make sure the chart is for women and the right category.
  • Compare your body measurements with the brand's guide.
  • Check the exact style's product page and stated cut.
  • Use the model's height as context, not a size rule.
  • Test the jacket with the layer and movements you expect.
  • Do not directly convert a Zara size into a Mango size.

Frequently asked questions

Do Zara and Mango bomber jackets fit the same?

There is no universal conversion. Zara says fit varies by style, cut and fabric; Mango recommends comparing each item's measurements with your own.

What size is the Mango bomber model wearing?

The wool bomber page checked for this guide says the model wears size S and is 5 ft 10 in tall. That describes one model and one product, not every shopper.

Does the Zara model show which size to buy?

The suede-effect bomber page checked here shows a 176 cm model but does not state her size. Use the product's measurement guide to choose.

Should I size up to wear a jumper underneath?

Not automatically. Try the jacket over the layer you plan to wear and check chest, shoulders and sleeves; sizing up can affect other areas.
