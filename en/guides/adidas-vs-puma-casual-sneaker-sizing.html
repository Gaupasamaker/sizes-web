Adidas vs PUMA: casual sneaker sizing.

We compare both brands’ official guides and use Samba OG and Palermo as specific examples when checking each product page.

Updated 9 Nov 20269 min readAdults · Casual footwear
Key idea

Measure your foot and use the official chart for the right category in each brand. Then check the exact Samba OG or Palermo product page. A matching EU label does not prove that the shoes will fit the same.

This guide compares Adidas and PUMA as brands. Samba OG and Palermo are examples for reading their product pages; they are not equivalent models or a test of which brand runs larger. Collections and product pages can change, so reopen the guides before buying.

What this guide compares

We focus on adult casual sneakers and the public information on official guides and product pages. We are not comparing cushioning or sports performance: this is about measurements and everyday fit. On each product page, confirm whether the size chart is for men, women or a unisex style.

Brands can present EU, UK and US sizes with different increments and conversions. Some charts connect a size with foot length; others focus on international conversion. Read each chart’s headings and instructions. Do not convert an Adidas size to PUMA using only the label number.

Adidas: pair the chart with model-specific guidance

The official Adidas size guide lets you check footwear charts by category and length. Measure your foot using its instructions and make sure you are in the right section. The official Samba guide describes the Samba OG as a regular fit and notes it may feel slightly narrow for some people with wider feet. Adidas suggests considering half a size up in that case. This observation is specific to Samba; do not apply it to every Adidas trainer.

On the Samba OG product page, check the colour, category, available sizes and any advice shown for that variant. Foot shape and how the shoe feels when walking also matter to personal fit.

PUMA: use its chart and check the variant

The official PUMA size guide separates footwear charts by category and provides international conversions. Choose the right column and confirm which system the shop uses. If the chart does not include the centimetre length you need, follow PUMA’s own measurement method rather than guessing from a similar-looking number.

The Palermo Leather unisex product page identifies the style and variant, lists materials and links to the size guide beside the selector. Check the exact version: different Palermo styles can vary in construction or availability; details on one page do not automatically apply to all of them.

Measure both feet before comparing labels

Measure at the end of the day, or when your feet are at their usual size. Place your heel against a wall on a sheet of paper, mark your longest toe and measure from the paper’s edge to the mark. Repeat for both feet and keep both results. Wear the socks you would normally choose with the shoes if relevant.

Enter the measurement into each brand’s chart separately. If one chart uses centimetres and another takes you to an EU, UK or US conversion, do not assume two matching numbers mean the same internal room. The chart helps you start; the exact product page and a fit check complete the decision.

Check the fit of a casual trainer

Try on both shoes and lace them as you normally would. Standing up, check that your toes can move and do not hit the front when you walk, your heel stays in place without rubbing and there is no pressure along the sides. Take a few steps, turn and flex your foot on a safe surface.

Notice the shape over the instep and room at the forefoot. Samba OG and Palermo have different constructions; their names and silhouettes cannot predict how they will feel on every foot. If one area presses, first review the shape, lacing and options for that variant before changing size automatically.

If you are between sizes

Measure both feet again and verify the chart category. Then read the fit advice on the exact product page. If the issue is width rather than length, going up a size may add length without relieving the pressure. Check whether the style comes in another width or shape.

When possible, try the shoes within the retailer’s return window and conditions. Save the brand, full model name, size, variant and how the toe, sides and heel felt. That gives you a specific Samba OG or Palermo reference without turning it into a conclusion about the entire brand.

Before you choose

  • Measure both feet and keep both lengths.
  • Open each brand’s official chart for the right category.
  • Read EU, UK, US and centimetres using that chart’s own headings.
  • Check fit notes for the exact model and variant.
  • Check toes, instep, sides and heel while walking.
  • Save brand, model, size and variant as one reference.

The most useful comparison does not automatically convert labels: it helps you check what each chart measures and what the exact product page says. Let your foot measurement, the model and a movement check guide your choice.

Frequently asked questions

Do Adidas and PUMA use the same shoe sizes?

There is no universal conversion that guarantees the same fit. Check each brand’s official chart, category and exact model page.

Which models are used as examples?

Adidas Samba OG and PUMA Palermo Leather unisex. They show how to read each brand’s information; they are not presented as equivalent shoes or as a winner-versus-loser comparison.

What size should I choose if I have wide feet?

Check model-specific fit notes and available width options. Adidas’s Samba guide includes a note for wider feet; review the current product page and do not apply it to every model.

Can I use my running-shoe size for these sneakers?

Use it as a starting point, then check the shoe type, chart and fit of the casual style. A familiar size does not replace checking length and width.
