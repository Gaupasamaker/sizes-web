Mango vs Zara: which size to choose in summer dresses.

The label is only the beginning. To compare two dresses, look at the cut, the fabric and the area with the least adjustment.

Updated 20268 min readWomen · Dresses
Our advice

Do not automatically convert a Mango size into a Zara size. Check the current product guide, identify the dress's critical area and compare bust, waist and hips with a similar style that already fits you.

What the official tools tell us

The official Mango size guide includes a dedicated dresses and jumpsuits category and links each size to bust, waist and hip measurements. Mango also explains that customers can open the guide from an item to compare their measurements before choosing.

Zara states on its official What's My Size? page that sizing can vary with each garment's style, cut and fabric. The brand provides a measurement guide on the product page and, when available, a recommendation tool based on body information and preferred fit.

Both sources lead to the same practical conclusion: there is no universal conversion that covers every dress. Review the garment you are buying today rather than a rule remembered from another collection.

Shirt dresses: the button line decides

On a shirt dress, bust and shoulders often decide before the waist. If buttons create tension lines, sizing up or changing cut makes more sense than hoping the fabric will give. A belt can shape a roomy waist, but it cannot create missing room through the bust.

Check sleeves and armholes too. You should be able to raise an arm without pulling the whole dress upwards. Allow real movement ease in woven linen or cotton. If the style is lined, make sure the inner layer offers the same room as the outer dress.

Wrap dresses: adjustable, not unlimited

The wrap can adapt to different waists and help when you are between sizes. The amount of fabric over the bust and the side seam position remain fixed, though. If the overlap opens while walking or the neckline needs constant adjustment, the tie is not solving the fit.

Check whether the skirt is a true wrap or only creates the look. A faux wrap may offer less flexibility than expected. Composition matters too: flowing viscose moves differently from crisp poplin even when both dresses share a silhouette.

Fitted dresses: waist height matters

Besides comparing circumferences, look at where the pattern waist lands. When it sits above or below your natural waist, the garment may crease across the torso or back. Changing size alters width, but it does not always correct vertical proportion.

If no size places shoulders, bust and waist well at the same time, try another cut. Sizing up for bust room may move shoulders and neckline out of position; sizing down for a defined waist may create strain around the zip.

SIZES tip: measure from shoulder to waist on a dress that fits well. This reference helps separate a circumference issue from a torso-proportion issue.

Flowing and slip dresses: watch hips and lining

A moving skirt can look roomy, but a straight cut still needs enough hip space to avoid riding up. On slip dresses, adjustable straps can correct neckline and torso length; they cannot add width to the garment.

Lining may be the least flexible component. If the outer dress drapes well but sitting feels tight, check the inner layer. Both brands offer varied blends and constructions, so composition and product information belong in the comparison.

Linen dresses: compare the full blend

Not every dress described as linen behaves in the same way. The balance of linen, cotton or viscose changes body and drape. A structured button-front style needs more accuracy through bust and hips; a loose design lets you choose more by preferred silhouette.

Do not buy a very tight size expecting it to “give”. Ease should exist during the first fitting. Raise your arms, sit down and check that seams do not pull. Linen's natural creases are different from tension lines radiating from a button or zip.

When you are between sizes

Write down the risk of each option. “The smaller may gape at the bust.” “The larger may move the shoulder.” If one issue is solved with a belt or adjustable strap while the other affects a structural area, the decision usually becomes clearer.

With non-stretch fabrics and lined dresses, prioritise the largest and least adjustable area. Wrap, shirred and adjustable styles offer more room to choose. If neither size resolves the proportions, change the cut instead of forcing a label.

Movement test before deciding

With tags attached, fasten the dress, walk, sit, raise your arms and turn your torso. Check whether buttons remain flat, the neckline keeps its position and the skirt drops back into place when you stand. Test it with the underwear you would normally wear.

Review the return conditions before removing tags or making alterations. A dress that needs a small hem adjustment may be a good purchase; one that requires rebuilding shoulders, bust and waist probably is not.

How to use SIZES

Save bust, waist and hip measurements in your profile. Then add a note linked to the brand and dress type: “Mango M, linen shirt dress, good at bust” or “Zara S, wrap style, adjustable waist”. This keeps useful context without turning one garment into a rule for the whole brand.

Final recommendation

There is no universal winner between Mango and Zara. There are dresses that suit your measurements, intended use and preferred silhouette more or less successfully. Check the current product information, prioritise the least adjustable area and test movement before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Do Mango and Zara dresses fit the same?

Do not assume they do. Fit may vary with the style, cut, fabric and specific garment. Check the current guide for each product.

Which measurements should I compare for a dress?

Start with bust, waist and hips. For a defined-waist dress, also check the shoulder-to-waist distance.

What should I do if I am between sizes?

Prioritise the least adjustable area and the least stretchy fabric. If neither size places the main seams correctly, try another cut.
