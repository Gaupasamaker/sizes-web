Uniqlo vs Zara: which size to choose in linen clothing.

A relaxed shirt and structured trousers can share the same fabric yet need different sizing decisions. Compare pattern, fibre content and current product measurements before transferring a size between brands.

Updated 20269 min readWomen · Linen
Our advice

Do not convert a Uniqlo size directly into a Zara size. Start with the garment type, read the fibre content and use the least adjustable area as your reference. The current product guide matters more than a general brand rule.

What the official sources can tell you

Uniqlo's linen collection includes shirts, trousers, dresses and skirts in pure linen and several blends. Its category distinguishes premium linen, linen blend and cotton-linen fabrics. That variety makes the composition of the current item essential.

Zara explains in What is my size? that sizes can vary according to style, cut and fabric. Product pages provide a measurement guide and, on selected items, a size suggestion based on body data and fit preference.

Neither source creates a permanent conversion between brands. Use them to choose the item in front of you today. Keep an older size as a reference, then confirm the current chart, pattern and composition.

Shirts: regular, relaxed or boxy

Both brands sell shirts with clean regular lines and designs built around extra volume. Terms such as oversized and relaxed already describe design ease. Going up again without comparing measurements can drop the shoulders, lengthen sleeves and add more body than intended.

Fasten the shirt and cross your arms. Buttons should remain flat and the back should allow movement. If you plan to wear it open over a vest, try that layer too. Compare chest and shoulder width with a similarly constructed shirt, not a stretch T-shirt.

Trousers: waist, hips and rise

Uniqlo's current linen range includes easy, straight and alternative-length trousers. Zara changes silhouettes, closures and proportions regularly. In both cases, the cut name helps, but it does not replace measurements.

Check waist, hips, rise and thigh. If pockets flare, the zip area forms tension lines or the fabric pulls when you sit, review the upper block. An elastic waist should hold without digging in and recover after stretching. With a fixed waist, decide whether a small alteration can help or whether the whole pattern is wrong.

SIZES tip: measure flat a pair of linen trousers that already fits. Save waist, hips, rise and length with a note saying whether the waistband is fixed or elastic.

Dresses and skirts: volume can mislead

A wide skirt can move freely while the armhole or bust remains precise. Shirt dresses depend on buttons and shoulders. Shirred dresses adapt more, but the elastic should not be fully stretched while you stand still.

In skirts, separate skirt volume from waist and hip fit. An A-line pattern behaves differently from a straight one. When a garment is lined, make sure the inner layer also lets you sit and walk without riding up.

The linen percentage changes the feel

Pure linen can have more body. Cotton blends may keep structure while feeling softer. Viscose or rayon often adds drape. None of these descriptions predicts a size by itself, but they explain why two garments with the same letter can move differently.

Do not rely on a tight garment stretching with wear. Movement room should exist at the first try-on. Do not deliberately buy too large around assumed shrinkage either. Follow the item's care instructions and choose a size that works today.

The size letter does not describe proportions

Two M garments may relate to similar body ranges and distribute their space differently. A shirt can add torso width while keeping a precise armhole. Trousers may have a very wide leg and a short rise. Compare fit points rather than the total volume visible in a photograph.

Vertical measurements matter too. Torso length changes where a waist seam sits, rise changes how the hips fit, and very long sleeves may warn that sizing up will shift the shoulder. If the circumference looks right but the garment bags or pulls, proportion may be the issue.

Reviews, model information and fit descriptions

Reviews help most when they name a detail: firm waistband, roomy shoulder, short rise or fabric without stretch. Do not copy a size without knowing the reviewer's measurements and fit preference. Use the comment to identify the area you should check.

Model height and the displayed size can help interpret length and volume, but they do not replace your proportions. Combine that reference with the official chart, fibre content and a similarly constructed garment you own.

Common mistakes when comparing the brands

The most common is converting one letter directly into another. Comparing a relaxed shirt with a fitted one, or elastic-waist trousers with a fixed waistband, creates the same problem. Another mistake is assuming every linen blend behaves like pure linen.

Do not build a brand rule from one purchase. A useful SIZES note keeps the garment type, cut and fabric. That context tells you when an older reference genuinely resembles the next item.

If you are between sizes

Write down the concrete problem in each option. In a shirt, the smaller size may pull at the bust and the larger may drop too far at the shoulder. In trousers, the smaller may restrict the hips while the larger gapes at the waist. Choose the option that keeps the structural lines and allows movement.

When neither size places the key seams correctly, change pattern. A cut with more bust room, a different rise or an adjustable waist can solve what one extra size cannot.

Move before you decide

Keep the labels attached, fasten the garment and move as you would during the day. Raise and cross your arms in shirts; sit and step up in trousers; turn your torso and walk in dresses and skirts. Check the front, sides and back.

Seams should return to position, buttons should stay flat and pockets should not flare under tension. Confirm the return conditions before washing, altering or removing any labels.

How to use SIZES

Save body measurements and add garment notes: “Uniqlo M, pure linen shirt, relaxed fit” or “Zara M, linen-blend trousers, close at hips”. The size keeps its context instead of becoming a rule for the whole brand.

Final recommendation

Neither brand fits everyone “better” in linen. Specific patterns work better or worse for your measurements, preferred ease and intended use. Check the current product information, compare it with a similar garment and move before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Do Uniqlo and Zara linen clothes fit the same?

Do not assume they do. Fit depends on the pattern, fibre content and current item. Check each product's latest size information.

Should I size up when buying linen?

Not always. Prioritise the least adjustable area and make sure there is movement room without losing the garment's intended shape.

Which measurements matter for linen trousers?

Compare waist, hips, rise and thigh. With elastic waists, also check recovery and whether the pockets stay flat.
